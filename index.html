Safety of LLM Agents

From Single Agents to Multi-Agent Societies

Overview

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly deployed as agents that browse the web, call tools, write and execute code, keep persistent memory, and collaborate with other agents to complete long and complex tasks.

This shift from generating text to taking actions introduces safety risks that do not appear in chat settings. An agent may drift from its goal, take irreversible actions, follow instructions hidden in a web page or a tool description, or store harmful information in memory that affects future sessions. When many agents interact, new failures arise from their interaction itself, such as conformity, error propagation, collusion, and cascading attacks, and these risks can be further amplified in large-scale agent societies.

This tutorial provides a structured overview of this rapidly growing area. We organize agent safety along two dimensions: the scale of the system (single agents, multi-agent systems, and agent societies) and the source of the risk (non-adversarial failures that arise from agents' own behavior versus adversarial threats introduced by attackers). For each part, we cover representative risks, evaluation methods, and mitigation strategies, and we highlight open problems.

Outline

The tutorial is 3.5 hours long, including a 30-minute break, and covers agent safety at three scales: single agents, multi-agent systems, and large-scale agent societies.
